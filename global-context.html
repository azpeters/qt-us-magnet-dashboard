U.S. vs. Allies vs. China — Global Magnet Production Context

The 17 companies tracked in this dashboard are rebuilding a domestic magnet industry from a near-zero base. This page places that buildout in global context: how much the U.S. and its allies produce today relative to China, how that gap is projected to close through 2030, and how U.S. capacity is distributed across the individual stages of the supply chain — mining, separation, alloying, and finished magnets.

Shown as share of the combined China + US + Allies subtotal (the report does not size a separate "rest of world" bucket for 2030) — not a claim about total global production. The report frames this same relationship as "US + Allies ≈ 20–22% of China's 2030 output."

Read this carefully: the 2030 figures are company-stated and government-projected targets, not achieved production. Historical rare earth project execution rates suggest 30–50% timeline slippage is common across this industry — the ~20% non-China milestone is achievable on paper but far from guaranteed. Notably, if the U.S. buildout executes on schedule, it — not the allied bloc — becomes the larger non-Chinese producer by 2030 (~40,000+ mt/yr vs. allies' ~28,000–32,000 mt/yr), a reversal from today.
