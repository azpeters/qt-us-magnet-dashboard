About This Dashboard

This dashboard tracks all known participants in the U.S. domestic rare earth permanent magnet supply chain, from primary mining through finished NdFeB and SmCo magnet manufacturing. It is maintained by QT Magnetic Solutions and updated as significant developments occur. The current edition reflects information available through June 2026.

Scope

Companies are included if they have active U.S. operations or U.S.-sited projects at any stage of the rare earth permanent magnet supply chain: upstream (mining, beneficiation, hydrometallurgy, end-of-life recycling), midstream (separation, REE oxide production, reduction/metallization, alloy and powder manufacture), or downstream (magnet manufacture, assembly manufacture). Companies involved only in end-use product assembly or distribution without magnet manufacturing are excluded.

Niron Magnetics is included as a competitive analysis subject only — it is not a rare earth company, but its iron nitride technology competes directly with NdFeB rare earth magnets in downstream applications.

Stage Maturity Framework

Stage maturity ratings are assigned per company per pipeline stage: Commercial (operating at commercial scale with revenue), Commissioning (facility built, in startup/ramp phase), Pilot/Demo (small-scale or demonstration operations underway), Development (facility or process under development; no commercial output), Announced (intent stated, no development underway), Paused/Suspended (operations halted; future uncertain).

Key Policy Context

DFARS 252.225-7052 — The Department of Defense is prohibited from procuring items containing permanent magnets sourced from China, North Korea, Russia, or Iran beginning January 1, 2027. This regulation is the primary near-term demand catalyst for domestically produced NdFeB and SmCo magnets. Several companies in this dashboard are actively building DFARS-compliant supply chains ahead of this deadline.

Critical minerals designation — Neodymium, praseodymium, dysprosium, and terbium are all designated as critical minerals by the U.S. Geological Survey. China currently produces over 85% of global rare earth oxides, over 90% of rare earth metals, and over 90% of finished NdFeB permanent magnets. The companies in this dashboard collectively represent the current U.S. effort to build a domestic alternative.
